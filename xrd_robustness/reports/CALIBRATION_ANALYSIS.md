# Calibration analysis of Dynamic ERM and JS consistency

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Status:** completed post-hoc secondary reliability audit of frozen models. No model retraining, checkpoint selection, seed selection, profile selection, or hyperparameter tuning was performed.

## Scientific question

The primary study asks whether consistency regularization between two measurement views of the same parent crystal improves robustness to PXRD measurement variation.

Because the classifier outputs a complete probability distribution, the frozen models support a second question:

> Does measurement-view consistency also improve the reliability of predictive probabilities under measurement shift?

This question was evaluated only after the main training and evaluation protocol had been frozen. It therefore remains a secondary result rather than a model-selection criterion or a separate primary method contribution.

For simulated Test, per-sample probabilities were regenerated by forward inference from the already frozen checkpoints on the already frozen evaluation panel/cache when probability files were not already present. No training or tuning occurred. CNRS used the already stored zero-shot probability predictions.

The original local `raw_results.json` was not available to the canonical audit, so the new public evidence set should be treated as the authoritative calibration/reliability readout. `summary.json` records `frozen_ece_reproduction.comparisons = 0`; therefore no claim is made that every regenerated condition was directly cross-checked against the old local raw file.

## Metrics and reading rule

The audit reports:

- **ECE**: 15 equal-width confidence bins; confidence is the maximum predicted class probability;
- **NLL**: negative log-likelihood, a proper scoring rule;
- **multiclass Brier score**: a proper scoring rule over the full class-probability vector;
- mean confidence and predictive entropy as descriptive probability-shape statistics;
- Macro-F1 and accuracy to verify that improved reliability is not obtained by sacrificing classification performance.

Lower ECE, NLL, and Brier are better. Mean confidence by itself is not a quality metric; it is interpreted relative to accuracy and the proper scoring rules.

The key reading rule is:

> Lower ECE alone can be produced by generic confidence shrinkage. A stronger probabilistic-reliability conclusion requires ECE, NLL, and Brier to improve together while predictive performance is maintained or improved.

## Simulated Test

The frozen simulated-Test design contains:

- 5 matched training seeds;
- 3 fixed evaluation seeds;
- 12 fixed evaluation profiles;
- 2 methods: Dynamic ERM and JS Consistency.

This yields **180 matched evaluation conditions**, not 180 statistically independent experiments.

| Metric | Dynamic ERM | JS Consistency | Mean paired Δ (JS−ERM) | Better-direction conditions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Macro-F1 | 0.618282 | **0.671078** | **+0.052796** | 176 / 180 |
| Accuracy | 0.618242 | **0.672075** | **+0.053833** | 179 / 180 |
| ECE ↓ | 0.289934 | **0.204461** | **−0.085473** | **180 / 180** |
| NLL ↓ | 2.667772 | **1.632070** | **−1.035702** | 176 / 180 |
| Brier ↓ | 0.648412 | **0.518434** | **−0.129978** | **180 / 180** |
| Mean confidence | 0.907905 | 0.876093 | −0.031811 | lower in 151 / 180 |
| Mean entropy | 0.235102 | 0.319464 | +0.084363 | higher in 152 / 180 |

Relative to Dynamic ERM, the condition-averaged values correspond approximately to:

- ECE: **29.5% lower**;
- NLL: **38.8% lower**;
- Brier: **20.0% lower**;
- Macro-F1: **8.5% higher**;
- accuracy: **8.7% higher**.

Two observations are especially important.

First, ECE and Brier improve in **all 180/180 matched conditions**. The largest observed ΔECE is still negative (`−0.01288`), and the largest observed ΔBrier is still negative (`−0.01487`). The direction therefore does not depend on averaging away a substantial set of opposite cases.

Second, NLL improves in **176/180** conditions rather than 180/180. This is why the automatic audit label is `support_with_heterogeneity` rather than an all-condition proper-score sweep. Nevertheless, the mean NLL change is large (`−1.0357`) and accompanies higher Macro-F1 and accuracy.

### Interpretation of the confidence reduction

JS has lower mean confidence and higher predictive entropy on average. That means the JS model is, descriptively, less sharp and less over-confident.

However, the result is not well explained by indiscriminate confidence shrinkage alone, because:

1. Macro-F1 and accuracy both increase;
2. ECE decreases in 180/180 conditions;
3. Brier decreases in 180/180 conditions;
4. NLL decreases in 176/180 conditions.

If JS merely reduced all logits without improving the probability distribution, proper scoring rules could remain unchanged or worsen. Their broad improvement while classification performance also increases supports the stronger result-level statement:

> **Consistency regularization improves both robustness and probabilistic reliability under the evaluated simulated PXRD measurement shifts.**

This is a statement about observed predictive behavior, not proof of a general calibration mechanism.

## CNRS-318 zero-shot external experimental domain

CNRS provides a more difficult independent experimental domain. Target labels were not used for fitting, tuning, temperature scaling, checkpoint selection, or any other adaptation in this audit.

### Pooled results

| Metric | Dynamic ERM | JS Consistency | Δ (JS−ERM) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Macro-F1 | 0.191176 | **0.209119** | **+0.017943** |
| Accuracy | 0.200000 | **0.210063** | **+0.010063** |
| ECE ↓ | 0.682570 | **0.612420** | **−0.070150** |
| NLL ↓ | 8.319988 | **6.118566** | **−2.201422** |
| Brier ↓ | 1.433841 | **1.315606** | **−0.118235** |
| Mean confidence | 0.882570 | 0.822483 | −0.060087 |
| Mean entropy | 0.289702 | 0.441971 | +0.152269 |

Across the five matched training seeds:

- Macro-F1 improves in **5/5** seeds;
- ECE improves in **5/5** seeds;
- NLL improves in **5/5** seeds;
- Brier improves in **5/5** seeds;
- mean confidence decreases in **5/5** seeds;
- predictive entropy increases in **5/5** seeds;
- accuracy improves in **4/5** seeds.

This is stronger probability-level directional replication than the earlier ECE-only observation because two proper scoring rules show the same seed-wise direction.

However, CNRS remains severely miscalibrated in absolute terms. JS pooled confidence is still about `0.822` while pooled accuracy is only about `0.210`, and pooled ECE remains `0.612`. Therefore:

> **JS substantially reduces over-confidence and improves probability quality relative to ERM on CNRS, but does not solve broad zero-shot Sim-to-Real calibration.**

The CNRS evidence is best treated as external-domain directional support for the reliability effect, not proof that the experimental domain is well calibrated and not an independent statistical confirmation based on five fully independent studies.

## Mechanistic interpretation: plausible, not proven

Dynamic ERM requires each perturbed spectrum to predict the correct crystal-system label independently. JS Consistency additionally penalizes differences between the complete predictive distributions from two legal measurement views of the same parent structure:

\[
p(y\mid x_i^{(1)}) \approx p(y\mid x_i^{(2)}).
\]

A plausible mechanism is therefore that same-parent consistency suppresses sharply view-specific probability responses along measurement-nuisance directions such as peak shift, broadening, texture, background, and noise. This could explain why the same training objective yields both:

\[
\text{OOD classification performance}\uparrow
\]

and

\[
\text{ECE, NLL, Brier}\downarrow.
\]

The present experiments do **not** isolate this mechanism causally. They establish the result-level association under the frozen study design.

## Claim hierarchy

### Supported by the current evidence

1. JS Consistency improves simulated-Test classification performance on average under the frozen evaluation design.
2. JS produces lower ECE in 180/180 matched simulated conditions.
3. JS produces lower Brier score in 180/180 matched simulated conditions.
4. JS produces lower NLL in 176/180 matched simulated conditions.
5. These probability improvements accompany, rather than trade off against, higher average Macro-F1 and accuracy.
6. CNRS-318 shows the same direction for Macro-F1, ECE, NLL, and Brier in all five matched training seeds.
7. The combined evidence supports describing JS as improving **robustness and probabilistic reliability** under the evaluated PXRD shifts.

### Not established

1. JS is not established as a standalone calibration algorithm.
2. A distinct or universal implicit-calibration mechanism is not proven.
3. CNRS confidence estimates are not well calibrated in absolute terms.
4. The 180 simulated conditions are not 180 independent experiments.
5. The five CNRS seed pairs are not five independent experimental datasets.
6. No conclusion should imply that consistency alone eliminates broad Sim-to-Real shift.

## Relation to the main study

The primary contribution remains the conversion of simulator-defined same-parent provenance into measurement-equivalence supervision for robustness under physically plausible PXRD variation.

The reliability result strengthens that story without replacing it:

> **The same consistency objective that improves robustness also yields probability outputs that are less over-confident and better scored by ECE, NLL, and multiclass Brier under the frozen evaluation protocol.**

For scientific measurement analysis, this matters because probability quality can support later selective prediction, uncertainty triage, repeated-measurement decisions, and expert deferral.

## Recommended reporting language

### Main-text / presentation wording

> Beyond classification accuracy, consistency regularization also improved the quality of predictive probabilities. Across 180 matched simulated-Test conditions, JS Consistency reduced ECE in 180/180 comparisons and multiclass Brier score in 180/180, while NLL improved in 176/180; mean Macro-F1 and accuracy increased simultaneously. The same direction was observed on the independent CNRS-318 experimental domain, where ECE, NLL, and Brier all improved in each of the five matched training seeds. These results support the conclusion that same-parent measurement-view consistency improves both robustness and probabilistic reliability under the evaluated PXRD measurement shifts, although it does not by itself eliminate the broader Sim-to-Real gap.

### Short slide wording

> **Consistency improves more than accuracy:** under frozen PXRD shifts, JS yields higher classification performance together with lower ECE, NLL, and Brier; CNRS shows the same probability-level direction across all five training seeds.

### Wording to avoid

- “JS is a calibration algorithm.”
- “JS proves an implicit calibration mechanism.”
- “CNRS is calibrated after consistency training.”
- “180 independent experiments all succeed.”
- “Consistency solves Sim-to-Real.”
